Finding Pages That Under-Capture Clicks: A CTR Opportunity Model Validated on Held-Out Clients

Unati Saini · FlyRank ML Internship Capstone · Lane 4: CTR / Engagement Opportunity Scoring

Abstract

Which already-visible pages receive fewer clicks than their ranking position would suggest, and can a model rank them better than simple rules? We use 26,375 pages from 38 clients in a pseudonymized search-engagement sample covering one 30-day window. A page is labelled a CTR under-performer when its click-through rate is below half the median for its position band, and a gradient-boosted model scores pages under 5-fold validation grouped by client. On held-out clients the model beat both rule baselines on precision@50 in all five folds (0.75 vs 0.44 and 0.42), a simpler version using only position and impressions still reached 0.73, and the model's top-scored decile held 68% under-performers against 27% overall. These findings are observational and directional: they prioritise pages for title and meta review, and they do not establish cause.

1. Introduction / Problem statement

Teams that manage many pages must decide where to spend limited review time. Pages that already rank and earn impressions but attract few clicks are cheap to improve, since a better title or description may help without new content. The decision this work supports is: which pages should get a metadata or content review first?

2. Data

Source. We used the ready-made CTR/engagement lane cut of the FlyRank ML Internship dataset (default_lanes/engagement_fix.parquet in the gated FlyRank/internship-lanes dataset), published alongside the pseudonymized warehouse release flyrank_pseudonymized_warehouse_release_v20260703 (exported 2026-07-03). It has one row per page: 33,202 pages from 44 clients, with 30-day totals of impressions, clicks, CTR, average position, sessions and engagement, plus content tiers.

Date window. The cut stores 30-day totals but no calendar dates, so the exact window cannot be stated. It lies within the release, whose daily data end on 2026-06-30.

Exclusions. (1) 358 rows with no CTR or position. (2) 4,766 rows with fewer than 100 impressions, because CTR on so few impressions is too noisy to trust. (3) 1,703 rows that could not be labelled, because in their validation fold the training-fold median CTR for their position band was zero or missing, so "below half of expected" is undefined. That leaves 26,375 scored pages from 38 clients.

Checks and privacy. CTR equals clicks ÷ impressions × 100 to within 0.005 points on every row. Client and page IDs are hashed, and no client names, domains, URLs or queries appear in this paper. Nearly all pages (99.6%) share one content type, so content type carries little signal in this sample.

3. Methodology

Assumptions. (1) With at least 100 impressions, a page's 30-day CTR is stable enough to compare. (2) Average position is a fair summary of how visible a page is. (3) The median CTR of pages at a similar position is a fair "expected" CTR. (4) Pages from different clients are independent enough that a client-grouped split mimics scoring a new client.

Label. Pages were grouped into eight bands by average position: up to 3, then (3, 5], (5, 7], (7, 10], (10, 15], (15, 20], (20, 30] and over 30, where each band includes its upper edge. The expected CTR of a band is its median CTR, computed on training folds only. A page is an under-performer if its CTR is below 50% of that expected value.

Features. Average position, log impressions, engagement rate, scroll rate, AI-traffic share, main search intent, and content-type, age, freshness and length tiers. We excluded CTR, clicks and every rule flag that was derived from them, because they would leak the label. We also excluded sessions, users and pageviews from the headline model, since they track clicks closely (see Limitations). Engagement and scroll rates are kept but are partly click-linked, because they are 0 when a page has no visits, so we also report a model that uses position and impressions only.

Validation. 5-fold GroupKFold by client, so no client appears in both training and test data. We used a histogram gradient-boosting classifier (depth 4, 200 iterations) and measured PR-AUC and precision@50 per fold.

Baselines. Two rule rankings on the same folds: R1 ranks by impressions, and R2 ranks by the dataset's own needs_ctr_fix flag (ties broken by impressions). That flag is a hand-tuned FlyRank product rule, so we use it only as a baseline to beat, never as a feature or a label. In this cut it is set for 97% of pages in the "page 1" position tier and almost no others, so it works mostly as a position filter rather than a test of CTR against peers. That explains how a model that never sees CTR can outrank it.

4. Results

The model reached a mean PR-AUC of 0.451 (R1: 0.272, R2: 0.252) and a mean precision@50 of 0.752 (R1: 0.440, R2: 0.424), and it won on precision@50 in 5 of 5 folds. The spread between folds is large (std about 0.18) because clients differ greatly in size and mix, so the ordering is consistent but the exact lift is uncertain. A model using only position and impressions, which cannot pick up the click-linked engagement signal, reached PR-AUC 0.421 and precision@50 0.728. Most of the gain over the rules therefore does not depend on engagement. Engagement features add a modest gain (to 0.451 and 0.752), and content features added none (0.401 and 0.660 when added without engagement).

The share of true under-performers rises steadily from 4.6% in the lowest-scored decile to 68.2% in the highest, about 2.5 times the overall rate of 26.9%.

5. Limitations & honest framing

  • Observational, not causal. The data cover a single 30-day window. We show association only and make no claim about how Google ranks pages or about the effect of any change.
  • Peer-relative, same-window label. "Under-performer" means below half the typical CTR for similar positions in this sample, measured in the same 30-day window as the features. It is a prioritisation proxy, not an absolute standard and not a future outcome.
  • Click-linked engagement. Engagement and scroll rates are recorded as 0 when a page has no visits, and visits come from clicks, so these features partly track the label. Several top recommendations carry ZERO_ENGAGEMENT for this reason. The position-and-impressions model (precision@50 0.728) is the leakage-safe reference.
  • Average position hides query mix. Expected CTR did not fall smoothly with position, which suggests query and snippet effects that this data cannot separate. CTR here is page-level, pooled over every query a page appeared for, which is why expected values sit below 1% even near the top. The same pooling may explain why impressions are the strongest feature: at a given average position, many impressions often mean a long tail of loosely related queries, which pulls CTR down.
  • Client imbalance. The largest client holds 32% of scored pages and fold results vary widely, so the lift estimate is imprecise.
  • Proxy leakage. Adding sessions, users and pageviews raised PR-AUC to 0.573, but these measures track clicks closely, so we report this as a sensitivity check and not as a result.
  • Narrow content mix and example cut. Nearly all pages are one content type, and the data are a pseudonymized example cut and not the full warehouse.

Expected CTR is the median CTR of the page's position band, computed on the training folds of that page's validation fold, so it varies slightly between pages in the same band (for example 0.44–0.57% for positions 5–7). CTR values are percentages.

Playbook. TOP5_LOW_CTR: review the title and meta description (snippet). STRIKING_LOW_CTR (position 5-10): rewrite the title and meta, and check intent match. LOWER_RANK_LOW_CTR: run a content review, then monitor. HIGH_VISIBILITY marks the top quartile by impressions, so fixes there pay off most, and ZERO_ENGAGEMENT marks pages where engaged sessions are zero. Re-measure after any change, and treat the scores as a reviewing order, not a verdict.

7. Reproducibility

The full code is in the notebook work/notebooks/w08_capstone.ipynb of the repository github.com/unatisaini/flyrank-internship-ml. The weekly notebooks in work/notebooks/ cover the data contract, leakage check, signal audit, model, validation audit and action playbook step by step. The tools used are pandas and scikit-learn (HistGradientBoostingClassifier, GroupKFold, permutation importance). Settings are random_state=42, GroupKFold(5) and a minimum of 100 impressions. The dataset needs gated access on Hugging Face and is not included here.
